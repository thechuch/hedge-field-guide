Split Station

Pump at the pond. Cartridge filters up at the tank. From your father-in-law's suggestion.

Where this came from

He proposed moving the pump and filtration up near the tiny home and running a long suction line, the way he did it at Lake of the Woods. The pump cannot go, because a pump pushes almost any distance but only pulls about 20 ft in perfect conditions and this one is rated 6. But the filters can, because filtration on the pressure side works anywhere. That gets him most of what he was after.

The tank station board

The filter bank shares the backer board and shade hood already planned for the distribution manifold. Two circuits, one board, and they must never be tee’d together. The fill circuit carries pond water into the tank. The distribution circuit carries tank water out to the zones. Same as the trenching rule: share the structure, not the pipe.

Winterizing the split

1. At the TANK station: strip the three sumps and dump them, cartridges out. Open the air / vacuum port.
2. At the POND box: break the two unions at check valve #1. The whole 85 ft line now gravity-drains downhill toward you and out into the gravel sump. Air follows in through the port you just opened at the top.
3. Low-pressure air at the tank-station air port to push the residue out of the bellies, downhill, to the pond box. Never at the priming tee: that is the suction side, the pump inlet is rated 30 PSI max, and the 51S01 is vacuum side only.
4. Open the pond box drain valve, break the suction union, pull the foot valve and line out of the pond. Pull the 51S01 bowl.
5. Unbolt the pond skid and carry it in, with both check valves, the cartridges, bowls, foot valve, and any gauge.

This is better than the single station on one point: the long run now drains downhill to the low end instead of needing to be pushed uphill to the tank. Air in at the high end, water out at the low end, which is the direction gravity already wants.

Honest downsides

Two stations to visit in the fall instead of one, and they are 85 ft apart. Not hard, but it is two trips and two things to forget.

The buried line carries 100 micron water instead of 5 micron. At 3 GPM in 1 in pipe the velocity is only about 1.1 ft/s, which is under the roughly 2 ft/s it takes to keep fines moving, so some silt will settle in the line. The annual blow-out is what clears it. Keeping the spin-down at the pond is what keeps this from being a real problem.

Pressure at the filters drops by the elevation gain plus a little friction, so call it 45 to 50 PSI instead of 55. The housings are rated to 125, so this is fine, it is just worth knowing.

It is a change to a plan that already worked. The single station was not broken. This is a convenience trade, and it is a good one, but do not let it stall the build.

Before deciding

· Measure pond August low water up to where the pump inlet would sit. Still the number that gates everything, and still unmeasured.
· Measure the elevation from the pond up to the tank. It sets the pressure left at the filters, and it is also the number that tells your father-in-law why his Lake of the Woods setup worked and this one would not.
· Ask him how much lift his line had. If that cabin was near lake level, 150 ft of suction is easy and his experience is real, it just does not transfer to a pond you climb away from.
· Confirm the tank manifold board has room for a second circuit before committing.
