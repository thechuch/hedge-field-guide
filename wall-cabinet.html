Stacked on the deck rail

Box Mounting

Two VEVOR boxes in the 36″ space. Nothing touches the tiny house.

Why the deck rail, not the house

No new penetration in a wall that has already had mold and water trouble. The rail is also an independent structure, so when the trailer flexes or you eventually move the house, none of this has to come off. It also puts the electronics farther from the greenhouse, which is a humidity source.

The cost is about 4 to 5 ft of extra cable, which is roughly $30 and no meaningful voltage drop.

The two decisions this drawing makes

1. Inverter on TOP

The Tycon is passively cooled with a hard 122°F ambient limit, so it gets clean ambient air at the bottom. The inverter is actively cooled and fine to 140°F, so if it ingests mild preheat from below it shrugs. The reverse (400W of inverter exhaust into a passive controller) is the case that actually cooks something.

Cost: about 4.5 ft more 2/0 to reach up there, and the lower box entries sit ~9″ off the rock.

2. Offset it, do not align it

You were right that stacked vents will feed each other. The fix is free: the top box is 8″ narrower. Left-align it and ~8″ of the lower box's top stays open to the sky. Aim the lower box's exhaust out that open side and the plume never reaches the intake above.

Keep the 4″ gap as well. Belt and suspenders, and it doubles as the cable chase.

The 9″ ground clearance is the one soft spot

River rock is the good case: it drains fast and splashes far less than soil. But in a winter that runs weeks in the teens, snow can pile into that gap and the lower box's intake is down there.

If you want margin: raising the stack 6″ above the rail cap buys you 15″ of clearance instead of 9″. Your call on looks versus winter headroom. At minimum, keep the rock clear there and check it after the first big snow.

Structure

  1. 2x10 PT ledger, post to post. With 7 to 8 ft between 4x4s there is nothing behind the stack, so the ledger creates backing anywhere along the span. Four bolts per post, not two, because a hanging stack pulls hard at the top.
  2. Two vertical 2x6 uprights lagged to the ledger, running down past the deck edge. Each box bolts to solid wood at four corners.
  3. Boxes on the outside face so they cost you none of the 36″ of walkway and the battery box lid still opens like a trunk.
  4. Cables into the gap at deck level, in conduit or split loom, with a drip loop before every gland. The lower box's entries are near the rock, so keep those especially well protected.

Width check

The stack is only 23.6″ wide in a 36″ space, so you have about a foot spare. That is where the DIHOOL PV disconnect goes, mounted beside the stack so it is grab-and-turn without opening anything.
