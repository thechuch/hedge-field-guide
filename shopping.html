Shopping List

What to grab locally vs what to order. Green bar = grab it this trip.

Do NOT buy yet

No ring lugs. No charger fuses. Both depend on measuring your bus stud size (M8 vs 3/8″ M10) and confirming your Iota model. Buying blind means wrong terminals. They are Amazon items anyway.

🛑 From the wiring review, 2026-08-02

The point-to-point verification found a missing grounding plan and several undersized or absent parts. These block wiring. See Panel Wiring for the why.

🔴 Blocks wiring, order now

  • Tycon TPDIN-CABLE-485 (~$25)The RS485 link. Cannot be substituted. The Tycon RJ45 carries +7V and +5V on pins 7 and 8; the TPDIN port is RJ12 and those land on its RS232 pins. A Cat5 cable destroys it. NOT the -232 version. It is only 48 inches, so measure your routed path.
  • Ground bar, lay-in bus (~$12)Goes in Box B. Everything metal bonds to it. This is the hub of the grounding plan that was missing.
  • 8 ft copper ground rod + acorn clamp (~$30)Was already on the list pending the Pegasus call. Needed now regardless.
  • 6 AWG GREEN INSULATED THHN, ~50 ft (~$45)Ground bar to rod, the single DC negative bond, and the Tycon GND. Green insulated, not bare copper: bare copper against aluminum rail or a metal roof corrodes galvanically.
  • 10 AWG green, ~10 ft (~$8)The EGC between Box A and Box B.
  • 20A single-pole breaker or inline AC fuse (~$20)The inverter AC output currently has no overcurrent protection. Terminal block straight to an outlet leaves the branch conductor unprotected.
  • 12 AWG for the AC runs (~$30)Inverter to the outlet, and grid into Box B.
  • Ferrule kit with crimper (~$25)For stranded conductors going into the Tycon screw terminals. A screw clamp on loose strands is the classic joint that slowly cooks.

🟡 Before the array goes up

  • 8 AWG PV wire, red + black, ~20 ftThe combined 3-string run from the Y-branch through the DIHOOL to the controller. 10 AWG is undersized. Per-string 10 AWG is still fine.
  • 3 inline MC4 fuses (check first)Read the panel label for "max series fuse rating." With three parallel strings a fault can be backfed at about 22A. If the label says 15A, each string positive needs a fuse before the Y-branch.

🟢 Should have

  • Manual AC disconnect for Box B (~$15)Small 2-pole DIN breaker upstream of the contactor, so you can kill grid power locally instead of walking to the house panel.
  • Paint-piercing star washers + 2 braided bonding jumpers (~$20)These boxes are painted and paint is an insulator. The jumpers bond each door to its body.
  • AL9CU dual-rated lug + antioxidant compound (~$20)For the array rail. Copper directly on aluminum corrodes.
  • Weatherproof 20A GFCI outlet + in-use cover (~$35)The office feed.
  • Multimeter if you do not own one (~$25)Required for the inverter neutral-to-ground measurement, which determines whether the GFCI will protect anyone.

Optional but worth it before the array is up

Surge protection, about $60 to 100 each: a Type 2 DC SPD on the PV side (Uc 100 to 150 VDC) and a Type 2 AC SPD on the grid feed. Rural roof array, and the Tycon has no ground fault detection of its own. If you buy one, its earth lead must be under 20 inches, long leads ruin the clamping.

📦 Amazon (do not hunt locally)

⭐ IOTA IQ-LIFEPO Smart Charge Controller (~$27)

Product code IQ-LIFEPO. Confirmed compatible with your DLS-27-15 (it is explicitly on IOTA's model list). Plugs into the charger's Dual Voltage Jack and gives automatic Bulk to Float to trickle for LiFePO4, instead of holding a flat 28.4V. Without it (or at least the free dual-voltage plug), the Iota only reaches ~85% and your grid backstop underperforms.

Check first: it will not work on DLS units with an internal IQ controller. Your label reads plain "DLS-27-15" with no /IQ4 suffix, so you should be fine.

2/0 cable + lugs, hydraulic crimper, adhesive heat shrink, terminal boots, 70A/30A charger fuses + holders, 6ga/10ga lugs, DC clamp meter (UNI-T UT210E), 40A DC contactor, snap-disc over-temp, DC thermostat, float switch.
