What changed from the old guide
  • Revision B (9 October 2026) replaces the four-monitor winter control design with an EV200 PV contactor gated by the TPDIN and an independent second layer. Heat moves to Phase 2, powered from the TP60 LOAD output.
  • The TP60 charge voltage is 28.4 V because its temperature compensation in lithium mode is undocumented.
  • The original controller in the photos is a 30 A TP30. The 60 A TP60 is a separate order. The 150 V TS60 was considered but was not selected.
  • PV strings combine in a protected combiner beside the lower cabinet. The old roof Y-branch and half-inch conduit instructions are withdrawn.

Combiner arrangement

  1. Pair panels 1/2, 3/4 and 5/6 on the roof. One inter-panel series link joins each pair. Label the free ends A+/A−, B+/B− and C+/C− after polarity verification.
  2. Bring each string pair down together. There are six current-carrying conductors plus the required equipment-ground conductor.
  3. Each positive enters its own MNEPV15-150-1PNP breaker before joining the positive combiner bus.
  4. The three string negatives join the separate PV-negative bar. Equipment-ground conductors join the PE bar.
  5. The combined pair enters the DIHOOL's top pair and leaves from its bottom pair. PV positive then passes through the EV200 cold-cutoff contactor ("+" stud on the array side) to the controller's PV+. PV negative goes straight to PV−. See the winter plan.
  6. The controller's BAT terminals connect to the battery bus with 4 AWG and an 80 A ANL. The LOAD terminals are used only in Phase 2, for the battery warmers. Never connect the inverter there.

Top-balance before paralleling

LiTime: fully charge each battery separately, then let them sit together in parallel 12 to 24 hours before use. A 0.5 V difference drives circulating current; over 1 V can damage the BMS. LiTime parallel guidance

  1. Somewhere above 50°F, charge each battery alone to full with a 29.2 V LiFePO4 charger of about 10 A. The same charger is the recovery tool if the bank ever shuts itself off.
  2. Rest each battery an hour and record its voltage. They should agree within a few hundredths of a volt.
  3. Parallel them on the bus: negatives first, then each positive through its fuse, positive last. Leave them 12 to 24 hours with no load and no charging.

How the cold cutoff works

A sealed DC contactor sits in the PV positive wire between the DIHOOL disconnect and the TP60. Its coil runs from the battery through two permission contacts in series. If either contact opens, or anything loses power, the contactor opens and the controller sees no solar.

Opening PV is the shutdown order the controller expects. The TP60 and its LOAD output keep running from the battery while charging is blocked.

  2. Test controls separately. Bench-test the TPDIN rule, layer 2 and the EV200 as listed in the winter plan before full charging is possible.
  3. Prepare the four batteries. Charge each one alone with the 29.2 V charger, check voltages agree, parallel them and rest 12 to 24 hours. Do not connect a mismatched or previously tripped pack into a live bank.
  4. Power the MPPT from BAT with PV isolated. Confirm system voltage and the full charge profile. Follow the final startup/precharge instructions for each load; no generic resistor or two-second precharge rule is approved.
  5. Inspect and commission PV. Before initial PV energization, a qualified installer verifies string voltage, polarity, isolation and protection against the released design. Keep within the recorded cold Voc and input limits. Enable initial charging only under the approved test procedure.
  6. Test the cold cutoff on the real circuit. With the DIHOOL open, confirm the EV200 follows the TPDIN rule (ice water on T1). Close the DIHOOL with the contactor commanded open: the TP60 must show no PV. Then allow it and confirm charging starts. Watch the first full charge: terminal voltage must not exceed 28.8 V. Prove loss of control power, failed probe, TPDIN reboot and network loss.
  7. Test the inverter branch and office outlet. Verify its protection and GFCI operation with the final neutral/PE arrangement. Increase load only within the approved branch rating.
  8. Record a loaded baseline. Check battery current sharing, voltage drop and joint/case temperatures at a stated load and ambient temperature. A hot joint needs investigation after isolation. Do not tighten live connections.
  9. Test winter and grid-loss behavior. Confirm heat can run from the battery bank and that charging stays inhibited until all batteries meet the restart conditions. Use safe test inputs; do not deliberately freeze-charge a battery.
  10. Save the final record. Attach settings, circuit drawings, torque references, measurements and test results. Recheck after repair or a part change. Follow the component maintenance instructions.
Normal isolation and emergency limits

For a planned controller shutdown, isolate PV first, stop the IOTA grid source, turn loads off, then use the approved battery disconnect sequence. Reconnection needs the battery-connected controller configured before PV is restored. The final labels must show every source and disconnect.

For smoke, unusual heating, swelling, hissing or a fire: move people away and call emergency services. Use an external disconnect only if it is safe to reach. Do not open or move a failing battery. PV upstream of its disconnect can remain live. Tell responders that both grid AC and a LiFePO4 battery/PV system are present.

The plastic storage box is not a tested battery fire enclosure. A loose lid, cement board lining or a handheld extinguisher does not establish containment of a battery event.

Freeze preparation and trench work

The intended winter arrangement uses roof rain to refill the tank while the pond skid is drained and stored indoors. Chicken water remains an active winter load. Insulation alone does not prevent eventual freezing.

  • Survey pipe grades and low points. Establish local burial requirements before trenching. Old fixed 12, 18 and 24 in depths are not a site approval.
  • Mark underground services and stage separate pipes before digging. Reserve a capped data/sensor conduit on a suitable route if practical.
  • Provide accessible low-point drains and a pressure-side air/vent port. Do not use the suction priming port for compressed air.
  • Before winter disassembly, isolate pump power and release water pressure. Remove and empty filter bowls. A blow-out cannot be assumed to empty them.
  • Store freeze-sensitive pumps, cartridges, bowls, gauges, foot valve and removable checks as their manuals require.
  • Any compressed-air procedure needs a regulator and a limit allowed by every connected component. The old phrase “low-pressure air” was not a complete procedure.
  • The proposed winter distribution drain-back path must be verified on site. Removing a check valve alone does not prove that all pipe and valve cavities drain.
  • Any heat trace needs the correct listing, thermostat, electrical protection and pipe/insulation compatibility. Add its demand to the power budget.
