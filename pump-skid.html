Pond Pump Skid

Pond to the 85 ft uphill run, on one removable board. To scale, 1 in = 11 px.

ANSWERED Two sides of the plywood: no, and you do not need it

It buys about 6 in and costs a divider frame, rear access, board penetrations, joints you can never look at again, and a board with no flat side to set down. And the premise was wrong, mine not yours: hutch height does not cost prime margin. The pump sits in the bottom row no matter how tall the box is, so every inch above it is free. Only the floor height and the pump's own elevation cost lift, 1 to 1.

The real answer is simpler. The 48 in hutch was always about 12 in taller than its own contents, and two of my numbers were too conservative. Same layout, same 36 in width, 36 in interior instead of 48. No redesign.

Five things this pass found that have nothing to do with height

1. The pressure side cannot drain without an air port

Pump low, up to the heads, back down to the drain: that is a 26 in inverted U with both ends low. No air can get in at the top (the pump has an internal check valve and four water-filled diaphragm chambers behind it), so opening the drain valve does almost nothing. Add a small tee and valve at the high point. A few dollars, and the winterization does not work without it.

2. Never blow air in at the priming tee

That is the suction side. The SEAFLO datasheet says inlet pressure 30 PSI max, and the 51S01 label says vacuum side only. Blow-out air goes in at the new high-point port, downstream of the pump. My earlier instruction to use the priming tee as the air inlet was wrong.

3. The blow-out cannot empty the filter sumps

A cap-fed housing is an inverted dead-end cup: both ports are at the top. Once the level drops past the cartridge bottom the air short-circuits up the core and stops moving water, leaving roughly half a gallon in each. Strip and dump the three sumps FIRST, then move air.

4. Break the check valve unions FIRST, not last

Check valve #1 is what holds the standing 85 ft column. The far end enters the tank top, open to atmosphere, so pulling those unions first lets the whole run gravity-drain backward out the floor penetration into the gravel sump. No compressed air, no pressurizing anything. It also clears the outlet stub, which is the one bit of pressure pipe below the drain valve and downstream of the check.

5. Filling the tank is a 9 hour run on an intermittent-duty pump

1,600 gal at 3.0 GPM is 8.9 hours continuous, and about 750 Wh off the bank. Nobody had done that arithmetic, including me. Check the SEAFLO manual for a stated duty cycle before you plan on filling from empty in one go, and expect to top off rather than fill.

Winterizing, corrected order

1. Break the two unions at check valve #1. The 85 ft run and the outlet stub gravity-drain backward out through the floor into the gravel sump, about 4 gal.
2. Strip the three filter sumps and dump them. Cartridges out, bowls indoors.
3. Open the drain ball valve into a bucket, then open the high-point air port. Now the riser and the heads gravity-drain.
4. Low-pressure air at the high-point port to push the residue to the drain.
5. Break the suction union, pull the foot valve and line out of the pond, let it drain downhill. A foot valve holds water in the suction line, so it will not drain until you disconnect it. Pull the 51S01 bowl.
6. Unbolt the skid and carry it in. Indoors: both check valves, cartridges, bowls, foot valve, any gauge. A diaphragm pump does not clear itself with a dry run.

Measure before you cut

· The 51S01 end to end and the check valve with its two unions. These are the two parts with no published drawing and they set the bottom band width.
· The spin-down ASSEMBLED with its 1 in adapters, not bare. Adapters add 3 to 5 in.
· Housing centers. 4.5 in bodies on 7.0 in centers leave a 2.5 in gap, and a 3/4 in nipple needs about 3.2 in after thread engagement. Dry-fit the three heads with real nipples and let the nipples set the centers.
· Pond August low water to the pump inlet centerline. This is the only prime number that matters, and the record has two conflicting figures (about 6 ft, and 4 to 5 ft above lowest summer water). Nobody has measured the drawdown.
· Pump port thread type. Listings split between 1/2 in NPT and 1/2 in NPSM, and the SHURflo unit this clones is explicitly NPSM. NPSM is a straight thread and tapered NPT brass may weep in it. Check before buying the 1/2 in FxF couplings.

Still to buy for this station

· 2 x 1/2 in FxF couplings for the SEAFLO ports, thread type pending the check above
· 2 short flexible jumpers for the pump ports, required by the manual
· Air / vacuum port: 1 in tee plus a small ball valve or plug
· 1 in barb x 1 in MNPT adapters for the check valve
· Unions: two at the check valve, one at the suction, one at the outlet
· Pump-start relay for the float switch (it is rated 1A, the pump draws 3.5 to 4A)
· 3/4 in nipples for the head manifold, length set by the dry-fit
· Rubber isolator pad under the pump feet
· Optional compound gauge (30 in Hg to +60 PSI) on the suction, which is how you see the strainer fouling. A vacuum-only gauge gets destroyed the first time the line sees pressure.
