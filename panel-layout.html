Panel Layout

Where each device goes on the removable mounting panels.

Your vents make this easy

Intake = side louvers (left). Exhaust = fan on the top face. So air sweeps in low on the left and out the top, and you are bringing cables in on the right, which keeps the cable bundle out of the intake. That is the right call.

So: put the Tycon in the left column, directly in the intake stream, since it is the only passively cooled device. Everything with lots of cables goes in the right column, next to the entries.

Both panels, to scale

Blue = airflow. Red = cable entry. Dashed blue = keep-clear convection column. ← swipe / pinch to zoom

Box B, bottom

Tycon gets its own full-height column

It mounts vertically with terminals down and it is the one device with no fan of its own, so nothing goes above or below it. In a 19″ panel it gets 4.7″ clear above and below versus the 7.9″ Tycon asks for.

That spec assumes still air. You have a thermostatic fan pulling a current right past its heatsink, which more than makes up the difference. Verify it: on a hot high-output day, point the laser thermometer at the heatsink and the box interior. Its ambient limit is 122°F. Well under means you are fine.

Right column, in cable order

Iota up top (its DC output and AC input both land near the entries), the IQ-LIFEPO module just below it (the cord is only 10″, and it can simply hang on the Iota rather than eat panel space), and the DIN rail low for the Baomain plus the second device you still owe yourself.

Box A, top

The inverter fills this box, and clearance is the tradeoff

At 16.2″ long it has to mount vertically: 16.2 of 19″ tall, 9.7 of 11.5″ wide. That leaves about 1.4″ top and bottom and 0.9″ at the sides, where WZRELB asks for 2″ all round.

Same reasoning as the Tycon: that 2″ is a still-air number and this box has forced ventilation. But this one is worth watching, because the inverter is your biggest heat source. IR-sweep the case on the first heavy load; it runs its own fans at 120°F and the case can reach 140°F.

Orientation: DC studs down so the 2/0 coming up from the battery box has the shortest run, and the fan end up toward the box exhaust so they work together instead of fighting.

Scope: this powers your office, not the house

What this system actually feeds

One circuit to one outlet for office equipment. Not house backup, not a load center. Your office keeps its normal grid outlets; this adds a separate solar outlet beside them.

The Iota tops the bank up at night if the panels did not fill it. The TPDIN watches battery voltage and closes the contactor only when needed, which is what keeps the system solar-first instead of letting grid do the work.

Capacity is generous here. An office load of 100 to 300W against ~8 kWh usable and 1500W of array is multiple days of autonomy.

The ATS is out, and the reason is not just space

1. Nothing needs source selection. You have two outlets, so you transfer by choosing which one to plug into.

2. It could not do the job anyway. This class of ATS has a 20 to 30 second transfer delay. If the battery ran low mid-workday the outlet would die and come back 25 seconds later, which means your computer already crashed. If riding through a dropout matters, that is a small UPS on the solar outlet, not an ATS.

For the record it was never redundant with the contactor (contactor = when to charge, ATS = which source feeds a load). It is just the wrong tool for this build. If it is ever revived, feed the Iota from the grid side, upstream of it, or an outage would have it charging the battery from the battery.

Switch the inverter off when you are not at the desk

A 3000W inverter idles 20 to 50W. Against a 200W office load that is a big proportional overhead, and left on around the clock for an outlet you use 8 hours a day it burns ~0.5 kWh/day doing nothing. The 80A breaker is your switch, and you are already walking over to plug in.

The outlet itself: a weatherproof GFCI in a wet-location box with an in-use cover is the fast, no-penetration version. An interior outlet is cleaner but needs a wall penetration (or better, come up through the floor if the office is over a crawlspace). An extension cord through a window is not a permanent wiring method.

Never tie this into the house panel. Keep it a standalone island: inverter to its own outlet, full stop.

The shelf

Cut it down, and only use it in the right column

It is full panel width, and installed as-is in Box B it would slice straight through the Tycon's convection column. Cut it to about 10″ and mount it in the right column only, where it makes a solid seat for the Iota (or the ATS later). Nothing needs a shelf in Box A.

One thing to check on the stack

Your lower box exhausts out its top face, and the upper box is sitting close above it. That fan needs somewhere to discharge. Look at where the fan sits on that top face: if the upper box covers it, open the gap with spacers or shift the upper box sideways so the fan blows to open air. A fan discharging into a 1″ dead space moves very little.
