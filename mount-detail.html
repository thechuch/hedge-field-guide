Mount Detail

How the ledger, uprights, and boxes bolt together and into the deck.

The idea in one line

Catch the frame at two heights. The ledger takes the top, the rim joist takes the bottom. Hanging the uprights off the ledger alone would be a 44″ cantilever carrying 60 lb, which is springy in wind. Two attachment points about 33″ apart makes it a braced frame instead.

The rim joist is continuous, so unlike the 4x4 posts (7 to 8 ft apart) you can bolt to it anywhere.

Lengths include 2″ of margin over the bare stack thickness, so you have room for washers, a lock nut, and any lumber that runs thick. Dry-stack and measure before you buy, and hacksaw off excess thread if a bolt runs long.

Two things that matter more than the rest

1. Through-bolt the ledger to the posts, do not lag it. The boxes hang outboard, so the load tries to pull the top of the ledger away from the post. That is withdrawal, exactly the direction lag screws are weakest in. A through-bolt with a washer and nut on the far side cannot pull out. Two per post, separated vertically so they act as a couple.

2. Hot-dip galvanized or stainless only. Modern pressure-treated lumber (ACQ / copper azole) is corrosive to plain and electro-galvanized steel. Standard zinc hardware rusts from the inside over a few wet seasons. It is the classic way a deck connection quietly fails years later.

Build order

  1. Check the rim joist. Confirm it is solid, measure how far its face sits behind the post face (I assumed 3.5″), and see whether the decking overhangs enough to need a notch. Shim the spacer to your real offset so the uprights end up plumb.
  2. Ledger up first. Level it, clamp it, drill 1/2″ through ledger and post together, bolt it. Two per post.
  3. Spacer and blocking at the rim, set in the same vertical plane as the ledger face.
  4. Uprights. Set the spacing to match the box mounting holes, not a round number. If the two boxes differ, add short horizontal 2x4 cross-blocks between the uprights at each box's hole heights and bolt the boxes to those.
  5. Hang the lower box first, then the upper. Easier to work up, and you can check the 4″ gap as you go.

Picture this before you build

The stack projects about 18″ from the rim joist (3.5 post + 1.5 ledger + 1.5 upright + 11.8 box), with the bottom about 9″ off the rock. Nobody walks there, but it is a real overhang.

You could save 1.5″ by half-lapping the uprights into the ledger instead of face-mounting. More cutting for not much gain.

Load check

About 100 to 130 lb total. A 2x10 on edge spanning 8 ft carries that without noticing, and this bolt pattern is far past what it needs. Strength was never the question, having something solid to bolt to was. That is what the ledger and the rim joist connection solve.
