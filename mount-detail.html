Mount Detail

How the ledger, uprights, and boxes bolt together and into the deck.

The idea in one line

Catch the frame at two heights. The ledger takes the top, the rim joist takes the bottom. Hanging the uprights off the ledger alone would be a 44″ cantilever carrying 60 lb, which is springy in wind. Two attachment points about 30″ apart makes it a braced frame instead.

The rim joist is continuous, so unlike the 4x4 posts (7 to 8 ft apart) you can bolt to it anywhere.

Two things that matter more than the rest

1. Through-bolt the ledger to the posts, do not lag it. The boxes hang outboard, so the load tries to pull the top of the ledger away from the post. That is withdrawal, which is exactly the direction lag screws are weakest in. A through-bolt with a washer and nut on the far side cannot pull out. Two bolts per post, separated vertically, so they act as a couple.

2. Hot-dip galvanized or stainless only. Modern pressure-treated lumber (ACQ / copper azole) is corrosive to plain and electro-galvanized steel. Standard zinc hardware will rust out from the inside over a few wet seasons. This is the classic way a deck connection quietly fails.

Build order

  1. Check the rim joist. Confirm it is solid, find how far its face sits behind the post face (I assumed 3.5″), and check whether the decking overhangs enough to need a notch. Shim the spacer to whatever that real offset is, so the uprights end up plumb.
  2. Ledger up first. Level it, clamp it, drill 1/2″ through both ledger and post, bolt it. Two per post.
  3. Spacer and blocking at the rim, in the same vertical plane as the ledger face.
  4. Uprights. Set the spacing to match the box mounting holes, not to a round number. If the two boxes have different hole spacing, add short horizontal 2x4 cross-blocks between the uprights at each box's hole heights and bolt the boxes to those.
  5. Hang the lower box first, then the upper. Easier to work down to up, and you can check the 4″ gap as you go.

Two things to picture before you build

The stack projects about 18″ from the rim joist (3.5 post + 1.5 ledger + 1.5 upright + 11.8 box). That is a real overhang out over the rock, with the bottom about 9″ up. Nobody walks there, but picture it before you commit.

You can save 1.5″ by half-lapping the uprights into the ledger instead of face-mounting, if the projection bothers you. It is more cutting for not much gain.

Load check

About 100 to 130 lb total. A 2x10 on edge spanning 8 ft carries that without noticing, and the bolt pattern above is far past what it needs. The strength was never the question, having something solid to bolt to was. That is what the ledger and the rim joist connection solve.
