Electrical safety

Grounding, bonding and the shared AC branch

Status: layout proposal. Field verification and final wire sizes are still required. No array-frame or solar-cabinet bonds have been installed. The earlier independent-ground-rod and automatic DC-negative-bond instructions are withdrawn.

The reported grid supply

  1. Shop panel supplies a 50 A receptacle.
  2. The supply follows an underground route of about 20 to 25 ft to the tiny-house 50 A inlet.
  3. The tiny-house panel supplies one shared exterior-outlet branch.
  4. The middle exterior outlet will supply the IOTA. Its route to the charger is about 15 ft.

A ground rod is reported at the supply end of the 50 A connection. The installed equipment-grounding conductor, panel arrangement and actual service neutral-bond point still need verification. Do not assume that the shop panel is the service equipment. A rod alone does not verify the return path for fault current. Grounding principles: OSHA.

The first GFCI protects later outlets only if they are fed through its protected LOAD connection. Schedule a functional test or have the installer verify coverage. This can interrupt the network and heater. Keep GFCI protection in service. A successful GFCI test does not prove equipment-ground continuity. Leviton LINE and LOAD explanation.

If the shared breaker trips, all three loads can lose AC power. A GFCI trip can have the same result if those loads use its LOAD side. Local battery heating and charge-temperature protection must keep their safe function when AC and network service fail.

Build one continuous equipment-bonding network

PE means protective earth. The proposed local equipment-ground bar is called EB1. Connect it permanently to an approved point in the tiny-house PE/bonding system. The electrician must define that point and conductor size after checking the supply, panel and trailer/chassis arrangement.

  • Bond both steel cabinets, their doors and conductive backplates.
  • Bond the metal combiner enclosure and applicable metal raceways.
  • Connect equipment chassis grounding terminals as each manual requires.
  • Bond the panel frames and rails through the exact approved mounting hardware.
  • Connect SPD earth terminals to the local bar with short, direct conductors.

Permanent array and cabinet bonding must remain connected when the IOTA cord is unplugged. Its removable cord cannot be the only permanent equipment-bonding path. The AC feed's equipment-grounding conductor also connects as required.

No need has been established for another rod at the cabinets or a separate 20 to 25 ft wire directly to the remote rod. If an additional electrode is required, integrate it into the approved grounding system. Soil cannot replace the equipment-grounding conductor. Do not reuse the old blanket 6 AWG instruction as a final wire schedule.

Unplugging the complete 50 A supply can remove the upstream grounding connection. Local equipment bonds must remain connected together. Independent inverter operation requires a verified fault-current path and a permitted neutral arrangement. Keep the inverter's separate office circuit isolated from grid power wiring.

Transient protection

Coordinated surge protection and EMP resilience

Status: placement proposal. SPD models are review candidates, not approved purchases. Surge protective devices, or SPDs, must match the circuit voltage, grounding arrangement, fault current and equipment withstand. No reviewed equipment has a verified complete-system EMP rating.

Confirmed route estimates are 15 ft from roof panels to the lower cabinet, 15 ft from the middle grid outlet to the IOTA, and 12 ft from house networking to the solar boxes. No PV descent cables have been run.

Proposed locations

  1. PV: three two-panel strings, then three separate 15 A string breakers, then the combined PV pair, verified manual disconnect, temperature-controlled PV interruption and TP60 PV input. Place SPD-PV1 in parallel near the controller, on the controller side of PV switching. Give it a short PE connection to EB1.
  2. Grid charger: existing outlet, approved supply cord and cabinet entry, AC connection with SPD-AC1 in parallel, charge-permission contacts and IOTA AC input.
  3. Battery bus: protected branches serve the controller, IOTA, inverter and auxiliaries. Any SPD-DC1 needs a separate protected parallel branch.

These locations are functional, not terminal instructions. Follow the selected device's circuit diagram. Do not pass charging or inverter current through small SPD terminals. Do not bridge a disconnect or charge-inhibit contact with an SPD.

The 100 V controller needs a coordinated PV selection

The ordered TP-SC24-60N-MPPT has a 100 V PV operating limit. Its data sheet mentions internal TVS lightning protection but gives no numerical common-mode or differential-mode impulse withstand. Obtain that information from Tycon. A surge device cannot correct an array whose cold open-circuit voltage exceeds 100 V. Tycon TP30/TP60 data sheet.

The MNSPD-115 brochure lists 180 V MCOV and 600 V line-to-ground VPR. MCOV is its maximum continuous operating voltage; VPR is a rated surge-test result. Its manual separately gives about 295 V at 100 A for an 8/20 microsecond waveform. Those are different tests. This device does not promise a 100 V clamp. Tycon and MidNite must confirm the application and residual-voltage compatibility. MidNite ratings; installation manual.

Keep active and PE leads short and direct. Include lead voltage in the protection assessment. Avoid coiled excess. Use an adjacent enclosure if the cabinet cannot maintain required clearances and wiring separation. Multiple SPD stages need manufacturer-supported coordination.

The 15 ft roof route alone does not require a second roof-end stage under this proposal. DEHN's IEC guidance uses 10 m, about 33 ft, as a review threshold; this is not a statement of local code. Reassess if the route changes or external lightning protection is present. Three separate roof strings would need three independent protection channels. Keep the strings separate until their breakers. DEHN rooftop PV guidance.

AC details that affect the layout

The DITEK candidate is a hardwired parallel SPD. It needs a proper junction or control assembly, not a splice into a loose extension cord. Its illustrated junction/control application requires short SPD leads and at least 3 ft of conductor from the connection point to the protected equipment. The upstream 15 ft outlet run does not meet that downstream spacing. Its instructions specify a 20 A breaker for the stated short-circuit rating; resolve the actual shared branch before selection. DITEK installation requirements.

The DTK-120HW data sheet lists NEMA 4X and 700 V L-N/L-G VPR. Those values do not establish IOTA surge compatibility. No ordinary indoor power strip is selected for outdoor service. An IOTA-only SPD does not protect every load on the shared branch. DITEK ratings.

The Eaton source-panel candidate requires a matching 120/240 V system and the specified listed breaker installation. It is not automatically suitable for inverter output. Source-panel and local stages need a coordinated design. Panel work belongs with the electrician. Eaton product data; installation practices.

Data, control and shielding

All-dielectric fiber removes the metallic Ethernet connection. Its endpoint power supplies still need protection. If copper remains, the TP-ESP-1000-POE candidate supports gigabit and PoE up to 57 V. Do not substitute the lower-voltage POE24 model because the battery bank is 24 V. Provide the required local bonds and outdoor weather protection. Tycon Ethernet SPD data sheet.

The TPDIN link uses a specific RS485 cable and an RJ12 connection at the monitor. An RJ45 Ethernet SPD is not a substitute. Exact TPDIN hardware and firmware still need confirmation. Do not add arbitrary suppressors to charge-safety probes. Their accuracy and open/short fault detection must remain valid. Local temperature protection must operate without networking. TPDIN WEB3 manual.

Use proper bonding and coordinated SPDs to reduce cable-borne transients. Keep a tested, disconnected reserve of critical electronics for recovery. A steel cabinet, SPD label, disconnected panel or loss of phone reception does not establish EMP immunity. No guaranteed direct-lightning or EMP protection is claimed. CISA EMP guidance.

Prepare each electronic spare

  1. Record exact model, quantity and serial number. Test normal operation before storage.
  2. Save its settings, connection instructions, test date and result.
  3. Switch it off. Remove every power, data, probe and antenna cable at the device.
  4. Apply appropriate electrostatic protection. Insulate exposed terminals. Use a tested shielding container or pouch and its specified closure. Add nonconductive inner separation where needed.
  5. Store dry, within the stated storage-temperature range. Control condensation. Keep reserves separate from operating equipment when practical.
  6. Record container identity, private storage location and closure check date.
  7. Recheck after use, moisture, damage or configuration changes, and at the intervals specified by the equipment and container makers.

A plastic case, unverified metal box or phone-signal test does not establish broadband EMP protection. CISA recommends disconnected electronic spares and Faraday storage. The priorities and quantities here are project proposals. CISA EMP guidance.

Required private log fields: register ID, model/serial, quantity reserved, function-test date/result, saved settings/manual, shield/container ID, storage location, closure/date checked and current status. E01 currently reads: owned; all protected-storage checks pending.

Inspect the affected system before connecting a replacement. Keep battery BMS, fuses, ground-fault functions and cold-charge interlocks in service. A working spare controller or inverter does not establish that the battery BMS survived.
