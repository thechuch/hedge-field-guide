Work you can do now

  1. Bench-test indoors. TPDIN firmware, password and cold rule; TP60 Lit settings on one battery with no PV; the EV200 opening when TPDIN power drops. Steps are in the winter plan.
  2. Top-balance the batteries. Charge each one alone with a 29.2 V LiFePO4 charger before paralleling. See battery box.
  3. Run the PV descent. Three string pairs plus a green ground in 1.5 in conduit, every end labeled, landed with breakers off. See PV wiring.
  4. Do the battery-box fire prep. Cement board on the power board face, shield between the box and the house wall, Class T fuse for the inverter leg.
  5. Send the two questions. Email LiTime with all four serial labels (built-in cold cutoff, heating pads, warranty). Call UltraHeat about 24 V side warmers.
  6. Send photos. TPDIN label, free space on the lower backplate for the EV200, and the tiny-house panel location for the bonding conductor.

What changed from the old guide
  • Revision B (9 October 2026) replaces the four-monitor winter control design with an EV200 PV contactor gated by the TPDIN and an independent second layer. Heat moves to Phase 2, powered from the TP60 LOAD output.
  • The TP60 charge voltage is 28.4 V because its temperature compensation in lithium mode is undocumented.
  • The original controller in the photos is a 30 A TP30. The 60 A TP60 is a separate order. The 150 V TS60 was considered but was not selected.
  • PV strings combine in a protected combiner beside the lower cabinet. The old roof Y-branch and half-inch conduit instructions are withdrawn.
  • An isolated ground rod does not provide the required equipment fault path. The solar metalwork needs a permanent bond to the approved building protective-earth system.
  • The standard LiTime batteries do not have confirmed automatic cold-charge protection. Both chargers need the temperature interlock.
  • Fans do not waive equipment clearances. Old fixed fuse, torque, cable-cut and precharge instructions are withdrawn where their basis was not confirmed.
  • The 80 A inverter branch does not support the inverter's full 3000 W rating. A separate branch design is required.
  • The old fire-response, battery bench-test and meter-based neutral-bond instructions have been removed from field use.
