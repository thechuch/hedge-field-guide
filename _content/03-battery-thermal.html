Work that can proceed with all power isolated

  1. Remove loose hardware and debris. Keep the enclosure dry. Inspect its base and drainage.
  2. Measure all four battery footprints and the available height. Include room for terminal covers, cables, sensors and service access.
  3. Provide a flat platform that supports the full base of each battery. Check sustained load capacity for the reported 180 lb combined weight.
  4. Check the Keter shelf in its actual orientation and with its actual supports. The proposed 1 inch front and rear overhang remains unresolved. A shelving-unit rating alone does not approve that arrangement.
  5. Keep cement board on a structural support if used as a barrier. Cement board alone is not a load-bearing shelf. Wood, composite decking and plastic require a support and heater-clearance review.

Raising the batteries above the floor does not establish winter insulation performance. Keep the support decision separate from the heat-loss calculation.

Battery buses, fuses and cable layout

Each battery positive needs its own coordinated source protection and isolation arrangement. Each battery negative goes to the negative collection bus. Cover all live studs and bus sections.

  • Use matching conductor construction and balanced total positive-plus-negative branch resistance for the four batteries.
  • Match state of charge by the battery maker's procedure before paralleling. Similar voltage alone does not prove equal state of charge.
  • Account for fault current from the battery and backfeed from the other batteries through the bus. The installer must resolve protection at every relevant source end.
  • Give the MPPT, IOTA, inverter, heaters and controls separate protected branches.
  • If a shunt is fitted, all battery negatives go to its BATTERY side. All load and charger returns go to its SYSTEM side. Avoid a return path that bypasses it.
  • Confirm bus stud size before buying lugs. Match every lug to the stud, conductor and approved crimp tool.

Unresolved: fuse types and interrupt ratings, holders, bus ratings, cable sizes and lengths, disconnects, terminal torque and precharge procedure. The earlier 150 A ANL battery fuses and 2/0 cable are inventory or design history. They are not a completed protection approval.

Battery torque values conflict across saved records. Obtain the instruction for the actual battery revision before tightening. Do not use an old page's number.

Winter operation

Heat during an outage, block unsafe charging

Design for review. This section defines the required behavior. The heater kits, contactors, sensors, interfaces and final settings are not released for installation. Do not wire from this functional description.

The heaters will use a separate battery-powered branch. Both chargers must stop if any battery is outside its allowed charging temperature. Heating may continue while charging is blocked, provided battery discharge temperature and remaining energy permit it.

Four required functions

  1. Measure every pack. Use an independent charge-temperature sensor on each of the four batteries. Cabinet air cannot establish cell temperature.
  2. Stop both charging sources. A hardwired permit controls the solar PV switching and IOTA AC switching. A TPDIN command must not override a hard temperature trip.
  3. Regulate each warmer. Each pack needs local temperature control and independent overtemperature protection. A warm pack must not stop heat needed by another pack.
  4. Preserve discharge limits. Low battery voltage, extreme temperature or a heater fault can stop heat. Extreme-temperature shutdown must also address the inverter and other loads.

Battery bus → protected heater branch → service switch → low-voltage disconnect → regulated 24 V converter → heater safety switch → four separately protected warmer branches.

Charging protection and restart

The proposed hardwired circuit uses four independent window monitors. Loss of a sensor, monitor power or control power must remove permission to charge. A manual reset follows each hard trip.

A 60-second delay is not a thaw time. A warm battery case can contain cold cells. Use a battery-maker procedure or a validated conservative soak method before allowing charge after deep cold exposure.

Proposed PV contactors open the two PV conductors while the MPPT battery supply remains connected. IOTA charge inhibition opens its AC supply. These control contacts do not replace service disconnects.

The exact PV switching assembly needs verified DC load-breaking duty, polarity, controller input inrush, precharge and coil interfaces. A requested OFF state is not proof that charging current has stopped.

TPDIN role and limits

The owned TPDIN model, V1/V2 hardware and firmware still need confirmation. Its documented temperature input and relay control can support normal operation. The independent hardwired temperature chain supplies the protective override. TPDIN WEB3 guide

  • Use a separate battery-temperature probe for the TPDIN. Do not share a passive RTD between two instruments.
  • Use a fused battery-voltage sense connection. Do not connect its 80 V maximum voltage input directly to the PV array.
  • Use suitable external sensing for high current. Keep inverter and charger current out of small inline current ports.
  • Use hardware contacts in series for required permission conditions. Software rule priority alone does not prove the required logic.
  • Keep normal grid-charge requests downstream of the hardwired permit. Use interfaces rated for the actual contactor coil duty.
  • Confirm the correct Tycon communications cable and the actual hardware revision before assigning ports, relay numbers or firmware.

Relay control, network monitoring and solar data are distinct functions. A LOAD-off command or temperature-compensation setting does not establish low-temperature charge cutoff.

Tests required before unattended use

  1. Bench-test the controls with high-energy circuits isolated. Simulate temperature with suitable test equipment. Do not test the system by charging a frozen battery.
  2. Test each pack's cold, hot, open-sensor and shorted-sensor conditions. Both chargers must stop in every case.
  3. Test control power loss, TPDIN loss, network loss, reboot, a stuck charge request and a held ARM button.
  4. Test each warmer, independent high limit, low-voltage stop and extreme-temperature shutdown.
  5. After power-circuit inspection, verify actual charging current stops for solar and IOTA, separately and together.
  6. Run a supervised cold-weather soak test with the final box and sensors. Record all four temperatures, heater duty, ambient conditions and energy use.
  7. Test one failed warmer and one displaced sensor. Confirm a warm pack cannot permit charging of a cold pack.
  8. Record the final settings, connection drawings and recovery procedure. Retest protective functions after wiring, sensor or firmware changes.
