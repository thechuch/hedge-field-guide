Work that can proceed with all power isolated

  1. Remove loose hardware and debris. Keep the enclosure dry. Inspect its base and drainage.
  2. Measure all four battery footprints and the available height. Include room for terminal covers, cables, sensors and service access.
  3. Provide a flat platform that supports the full base of each battery. Check sustained load capacity for the reported 180 lb combined weight.
  4. Check the Keter shelf in its actual orientation and with its actual supports. The proposed 1 inch front and rear overhang remains unresolved. A shelving-unit rating alone does not approve that arrangement.
  5. Keep cement board on a structural support if used as a barrier. Cement board alone is not a load-bearing shelf. Wood, composite decking and plastic require a support and heater-clearance review.
  6. Plan restraints that prevent movement and keep cables off the terminals. Do not drill the battery cases.
  7. Keep terminal and fuse areas clear of loose foam, foil and stored tools. Select any future insulation as part of the complete enclosure and heater design.

Raising the batteries above the floor does not establish winter insulation performance. Keep the support decision separate from the heat-loss calculation.

Top-balance before paralleling

LiTime: fully charge each battery separately, then let them sit together in parallel 12 to 24 hours before use. A 0.5 V difference drives circulating current; over 1 V can damage the BMS. LiTime parallel guidance

  1. Somewhere above 50°F, charge each battery alone to full with a 29.2 V LiFePO4 charger of about 10 A. The same charger is the recovery tool if the bank ever shuts itself off.
  2. Rest each battery an hour and record its voltage. They should agree within a few hundredths of a volt.
  3. Parallel them on the bus: negatives first, then each positive through its fuse, positive last. Leave them 12 to 24 hours with no load and no charging.

Shared exterior outlet circuit

The three exterior outlets share one tiny-house branch. The first, nearest the panel, is a GFCI and supplies the network stack. The middle will supply the IOTA. The far outlet also branches to the Rinnai water heater.

Confirm the branch breaker rating and the simultaneous load of all three uses. Include the heater's actual electrical auxiliaries. The 50 A house inlet does not establish this branch's available capacity.

Confirm whether the middle and far outlets are on the first GFCI's protected LOAD connection. Plan this test because it can interrupt the network and heater. A GFCI test does not prove equipment-ground continuity.

A shared trip can remove the charger, network and heater together. Local battery-temperature protection must continue to function without the network.

One cooling circuit per cabinet

The replacement Wathai 24 V fans are mounted on the plates. The new thermostats are reported as STEGO KTS 111 (verify the label): normally open, closing as the cabinet warms, which is correct for a fan. Rated 30 W at 24 to 72 V DC, about 1.25 A at 24 V, which covers a small fan. They clip to 35 mm DIN rail and use push-in terminals. STEGO KTS 111

Wiring the thin fan leads: stranded wire needs a crimped ferrule (8 or 12 mm sleeve) in a push-in terminal. Do not push the thin fan wire in directly. Make a short 18 AWG stranded jumper with an 8 mm ferrule crimped by a ratcheting square ferrule tool, push that into the thermostat, and join the other end to the fan lead in a WAGO 221 lever connector. Release a push-in wire by pressing its release slot with a small flat screwdriver.

Owned fan-supply candidate, PS-F: MEAN WELL DDR-30G-24. It accepts 9 to 36 V DC and supplies regulated 24 V. Its full output at 12 to 36 V input is 1.25 A, 30 W total. Check the combined fan startup and running loads, branch protection and temperature derating. DDR-30 specifications

Mount the DDR upright, with input terminals below and output terminals above. Keep 40 mm clear above, 20 mm below and 5 mm on each side. Keep it dry. Its isolated output return must remain separate from the input return. The installation manual prohibits connecting its primary and secondary sides together. DDR installation manual

Regulated 24 V supply → fan branch fuse → cooling thermostat → fan positive. Fan negative → regulated return.

  • Use one thermostat in each cabinet. It must close as the cabinet becomes warm.
  • Check each fan's label current against the thermostat's 30 W DC rating (about 1.25 A at 24 V).
  • Move the DDR-30G-24 to a second short DIN rail. It needs 40 mm open above and 20 mm below, and should sit apart from the MC-9b's 120 V terminals. The present rail is full.
  • Verify the fan's permitted voltage range. Its 24 V label alone does not permit a direct connection to a bank charging above 28 V.
  • Keep the recorded top exhaust and side intake paths clear. Confirm actual fan direction and cabinet temperatures with doors closed.
  • Keep cabinet cooling independent of battery heating.

Solar must run this winter. Revision B keeps the goals of the earlier winter design and cuts the hardware to parts that can be bought and built in weeks. Build it in three phases.

The cold cutoff must work before PV is ever connected. The batteries have no confirmed built-in cold protection, and the TP60 manual documents no low-temperature charge cutoff. A sunny morning after a frost is exactly when the controller would charge cold cells.

How the cold cutoff works

The EV200 switches PV positive between the DIHOOL and TP60. Its coil receives battery power through CH3 and a separate hardware temperature contact if selected. A confirmed BMS acts inside each battery. Loss of permission must stop charging; actual fault and restart behavior require tests.

AUX positive → proposed 5 A fuse → CH3 → independent hardware permit, if used → EV200 red coil wire. EV200 black coil wire → AUX negative

DIHOOL PV+ → EV200 main A1 (+) → main A2 (−) → TP60 PV+. DIHOOL PV− → TP60 PV−. Both large studs carry the positive circuit.

Opening PV is the shutdown order the controller expects. The TP60 and its LOAD output keep running from the battery while charging is blocked.

Phase 2: battery heat

  • Insulation: unfaced mineral wool such as ROCKWOOL Comfortboard 80 (R-4.2 per inch, noncombustible). It is allowed on every face, including lid and front, because it does not burn. Keep fibers away from terminals. In winter plug the low intake with mineral wool. Keep the high exhaust open at all times for hydrogen relief. Unplug the box fans for winter.
  • Warmers: UltraHeat custom 24 V LiFePO4 warmers, one panel on each long side of each battery, about 10 to 15 W per side. Sides only, never underneath.
  • LiTime caution: LiTime's self-heating page discourages external heating because a strong heater warms the case well before the cells. Its manual allows pre-heating. Low power and side mounting are proposals. They do not establish cell temperature. Obtain heating guidance and validate cold-soak recovery before charging.

TP60 LOAD output (S07 low-voltage cutoff) → 10 A fuse → TPDIN CH4 → per-battery fuse → per-battery high limit → warmer pair

  • Proposed CH4 rule: heat on below 46°F, off above 55°F. A warm sensed pack must not remove required heat from a colder pack; demand control or local regulation remains unresolved.
  • The LOAD output's low-voltage cutoff stops heat before it flattens the bank. Heater returns go to the TP60 LOAD negative terminal.
  • Each battery gets an independent over-temperature limit in series (normally closed bimetal on a pad plus a one-shot thermal fuse), unless UltraHeat confirms an equivalent built-in limit in writing.
  • UltraHeat's 12 V product thermostat holds about 35 to 45°F, below the 46°F allow point. Ask for a version without it or with higher setpoints.

Temperature logging: GW3000 and two WN34BL probes are ordered for battery-case monitoring. A separate third thermometer is for the coop. Box-air and outdoor sensors still need to be supplied. Alerts: battery case below 35°F and above 100°F. These are for watching only. No wireless or Home Assistant signal is part of the charge cutoff.

Use a suitable current-limited control test setup and simulated temperature inputs. Record hardware and firmware. Test cold trip, continuous warm timing, timer reset, open and shorted probes, full power loss, PoE power and software restart. Other rules and remote commands must not bypass temperature permission. What revision B gives up compared with revision A

Revision A used four Siemens window monitors with four probes, two Gigavac PV contactors, a manual ARM latch, separate control and heater supplies, a BatteryProtect and a separate control enclosure. Those parts are withdrawn for this winter.

  • Revision A watched every pack. Revision B watches one pack per layer, so T1 goes on the coldest pack, and the Ecowitt loggers check that assumption.
  • Revision A required manual ARM after a hard trip. Revision B proposes automatic restart after validated warm-up. The one-hour case-temperature rule remains unvalidated.

The revision A architecture and failure analysis remain the engineering reference for a future upgrade.
