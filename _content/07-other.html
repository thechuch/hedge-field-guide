Water system: where each line goes

The 1,600-gallon tank receives pond water in warm months and roof rain in cold months. A submersible pump sends tank water out through the top to the distribution manifold. The two bottom bulkheads are for drain and utility outlets. The mini barrel has its own pump for greenhouse irrigation.

Keep the pond-fill, rain and barrel lines separate. They may follow a common planned route, but they must not join through a tee. Electrical burial methods, separation and depth still need a site-specific design. Low voltage alone does not approve a shared trench.

This filter arrangement has no verified drinking-water treatment claim. The old claim that a second carbon cartridge guarantees glyphosate removal has been removed. Any treatment claim needs the actual media specification, contact time and water test results.

Pond station layout and the open split-station decision

The existing layout target is a single-sided removable 3/4 in plywood backboard in a 36 in wide × 24 in deep × 36 in high internal space. Front access is needed to remove the sumps. Dry-fit every part before cutting the final enclosure. The pump inlet should remain as low as practical, above flooding exposure, with a short suction route.

Distribution manifold and local control

The intended manifold has three active zones and one capped spare. Run one zone at a time.

  1. Zucchini irrigation.
  2. Trees and the chicken-waterer fill.
  3. Mini-barrel fill for the separate greenhouse pump.
  4. Capped spare, with a future butternut area under consideration.

Planned flow order: tank pump → check valve → drip filter → full-port shutoff → pressure gauge → manifold → zone valves. Keep pipe weight on a service board. Put electrical equipment above plumbing with suitable cable entries. Keep valves and unions accessible.

Low-pressure emitters remain the target. The pump's maximum head is about 10 PSI at zero flow; available pressure will be lower while water flows. Measure zone pressure and flow before buying all the emitters. The owned 3/4 in garden-hose-thread filter was allocated to the barrel/greenhouse line. The main manifold still needs a correctly sized filter.

Proposed valves are motorized, normally closed on loss of power, with local automatic return. Verify the exact model, voltage range, travel time and return-energy requirements. A nominal 24 V valve is not automatically suitable for a bank charging at 28.8 V.

The private irrigation configuration remains the source for GPIO assignments. Its recorded September update hides direct pump control, uses an on-device start delay, limits runs to one hour and enforces a 60-second minimum intended for the proposed valve's return capacitor. These are software records. The actual pump and valves still need bench tests for startup, stop, network loss and power loss. The IOTA contactor is reserved for the charger.

Freeze preparation and trench work

The intended winter arrangement uses roof rain to refill the tank while the pond skid is drained and stored indoors. Chicken water remains an active winter load. Insulation alone does not prevent eventual freezing.

  • Survey pipe grades and low points. Establish local burial requirements before trenching. Old fixed 12, 18 and 24 in depths are not a site approval.
  • Mark underground services and stage separate pipes before digging. Reserve a capped data/sensor conduit on a suitable route if practical.
  • Provide accessible low-point drains and a pressure-side air/vent port. Do not use the suction priming port for compressed air.
  • Before winter disassembly, isolate pump power and release water pressure. Remove and empty filter bowls. A blow-out cannot be assumed to empty them.
  • Store freeze-sensitive pumps, cartridges, bowls, gauges, foot valve and removable checks as their manuals require.
  • Any compressed-air procedure needs a regulator and a limit allowed by every connected component. The old phrase “low-pressure air” was not a complete procedure.
  • The proposed winter distribution drain-back path must be verified on site. Removing a check valve alone does not prove that all pipe and valve cavities drain.
  • Any heat trace needs the correct listing, thermostat, electrical protection and pipe/insulation compatibility. Add its demand to the power budget.
Shed freezer and chicken waterer: separate designs on hold

Shed freezer: Two additional LiTime 24 V / 100 Ah batteries are allocated to a separate 2P bank, about 5.12 kWh nominal. A roughly 800 W array and a smaller low-idle inverter are proposals. Final selection requires measured freezer energy, compressor-start demand, cold-charge protection, hot-weather limits and site solar estimates. An insulated cooler does not provide a low-temperature charging cutoff.

Chicken waterer: The record lists an owned DERNORD 24 V / 600 W element, a 1 in stainless bulkhead and horizontal nipples. The planned reservoir is about 5 to 7 gallons with a short drinking bar. The electrical heater design is not released. A fixed-resistance 600 W element rated at 24 V would be about 864 W at 28.8 V, so the old 25 A wiring schedule cannot be used unchanged. Select voltage regulation or a compatible heater, independent low-water and high-temperature protection, suitable DC switching and verified vessel seals before installation.

These loads are separate from the four main battery warmers. Their energy has not been included in the winter battery-heater runtime example.
