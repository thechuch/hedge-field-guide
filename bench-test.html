Bench Test

Power the inverter up safely to check fan direction, before you drill anything.

Why this is simple: one battery is already 24V

Your LiTime packs are 24V each, so a single one is a complete power source for this. No bus bars, no bank, no box wiring. At idle the inverter pulls only 1 to 2A, so you do not even need to make up your 2/0 yet. A pair of 10 or 12 AWG jumpers with ring terminals is plenty.

Do it on a bench, not in the box. You need the fan answer before the inverter goes on the panel.

Inverter bench rig

Red = positive, dark = negative, blue dashed = the temporary pre-charge jumper. Numbers are the order.

The sequence, and why each step exists

  1. Inverter rocker OFF. Nothing plugged into the AC output yet.
  2. Negative first. Battery − to inverter −, bolted down. There is no circuit yet, so this cannot spark. Always the safe one to land first.
  3. Pre-charge the capacitors. Bridge from the fuse output to the inverter + through a 10 to 100 ohm resistor for about 2 seconds, then remove it. This is the step people skip. The inverter has big input caps that gulp current the instant you connect, whether the rocker is on or off, so the inrush happens at connection, not at switch-on. An incandescent bulb in series works if you have no resistor.
  4. Bolt the positive for real. After a pre-charge it should be nearly silent.
  5. Now flip the rocker on and feel the fans.

Disconnect in reverse: positive off FIRST

Same rule as the main bank. With the positive removed, the negative is harmless to unbolt.

If you have no resistor or bulb: connecting straight will give you a bright pop. At 24V from one battery it will not hurt you, but it pits the terminals. Wear glasses and do not be holding the post when you make the connection.

What to check while it is live

  • Fan direction. Blowing out of the grilles, or sucking in? This is the answer that unblocks your gland positions. Whichever end blows hot air out is the end that mounts UP.
  • Remote port. With the case in your hands, look hard for a small 2-pin connector or an RJ11/RJ12 style jack on the front face.
  • Display and rocker behave normally, no alarm lights.
  • AC output is live. Plug in a lamp or a phone charger.
  • Idle behaviour. Listen for whether the fans run constantly at no load or only cycle on. That tells you something about how much idle heat to expect in the box.

Do the Tycon in the same session

This closes your riskiest open item

The Tycon ships set to "FLd" (flooded lead acid) and must be changed to Lithium, 28.8V, BEFORE the battery bank is ever connected. A wrong charge profile damages LiFePO4.

There is a chicken-and-egg in that: the Tycon needs battery power to configure. Which is exactly what you have on the bench right now. Hook the same single battery to its BATTERY terminals only, leave PV disconnected, set the profile, and disconnect.

The Tycon powers up from the battery side, so no panels are needed to reach its menu.

Bench safety, short version

Always fuse the positive

One 150A ANL in the positive lead. Even a single 100Ah LiFePO4 can push hundreds of amps into a short, and a bench with loose cable ends is exactly where that happens.

Clear the bench

Non-conductive surface, no metal tools or scrap lying under the terminals. Boot or tape any terminal you are not actively working on.

No jewelry, glasses on

A ring across 24V at these currents is a burn. Insulated tools, and keep the extinguisher in the room.

Do not leave it connected

The inverter idles at 20 to 50W and will slowly pull the pack down. Fine for an hour, but disconnect when you are done rather than walking away.

Order of operations this puts you in

1. Bench test the inverter and configure the Tycon → 2. decide inverter orientation from the fan direction → 3. drill the glands, including the two 1-3/8" holes → 4. mount everything.

Step 3 is blocked on step 1, which is why this test is the next thing to do rather than a nice-to-have. The 1-3/8" holes are the two you cannot easily move.
